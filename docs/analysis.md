# Analysis structure

Analyses are organized by scientific question. Scripts live in `scripts/analysis/` and write matching `tables/`, `models/`, and `figures/` folders under `outputs/analysis/`.

| Order | Topic | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `00` | Dataset overview and coverage | Implemented |
| `01` | Total catch and adjusted annual variation | Descriptive baseline and adjusted positive-catch model implemented |
| `02` | Joint relative species composition and species phenology | Implemented |
| `03` | Dedicated migration phenology | Condition-standardized species passage timing implemented |
| `04` | Age and sex demography | Standardized age-composition and adult sex-composition models implemented |
| `05` | Biometrics and body condition | Planned |
| `06` | Moult timing and progression | Planned |
| `07` | Recoveries and movements | Exploratory recovery, trajectory, and flight-altitude figures implemented; formal movement analysis planned |

`ringing_happened` means that at least one bird was recorded. The effort layer now identifies a small number of documented operated zero-catch dates, but quantitative net-hours remain unavailable. Analyses should therefore use “catch” rather than “abundance” until stronger effort data are reconstructed.

## Daily covariate pipeline

Covariates that are reused across scientific questions are produced before the response-specific analyses:

1. `scripts/intermediate/01_build_daily_context.R` assembles source-specific daily observations, deterministic moon variables, and ERA5 summaries.
2. `scripts/intermediate/02_build_mist_model.R` fits one three-state mist model from detailed observations and ERA5, then writes daily state probabilities under `data/03_intermediate/mist_model/`.
3. `scripts/curated/05_build_daily_coverage.R` creates the one canonical external daily table in `data/04_curated/`, retaining observed mist evidence, the unified state distribution, and explicit effort evidence.
4. `config/daily_covariates/operations_history.csv` stores page-verified lighting, nets, playback, vegetation, staffing, and interruption evidence. The daily builder materializes only reviewed mist, rain, net-site, night/dawn-operation, and nocturnal-playback values needed for count modeling; all other evidence remains in the history.
5. `scripts/diagnostics/summarize_daily_effort.R`, `summarize_daily_covariate_coverage.R`, and `audit_djp_team_size.R` report coverage without adding another data layer.

The total-catch workflow is under `scripts/analysis/01_total_catch/`: descriptive totals, covariate validation, model-data preparation, staged model comparison, documented-operations sensitivity, diagnostics, and one unified report. Swallow and martin catches are retained separately and excluded from this response because targeted daytime catching is a different process. The primary negative-binomial GAM standardizes positive-catch dates for season timing, moon, unified three-state mist, rain, wind speed, temperature, and pressure. Observed mist fixes the state where known; one ERA5 calibration supplies state probabilities elsewhere, and the catch analysis pools 20 imputations. Four post-1976 predictor sets, excluding the 1994–1995 bush-net transition, are compared with complete seasons held out. On 824 positive-catch dates in 36 seasons, M4-S is the M4 daily-covariate model with a smooth year term on the common operations subset, M5 adds a fixed back/front bush period, and M6 adds documented nocturnal playback. The bush-period contrast is identified only under the smooth-year assumption and may include other coincident changes. Team size is retained for exploratory coverage only; daily net deployment is evidence, not a historical configuration covariate.

The [unified daily-count report](outputs/analysis/01_total_catch/daily_count_analysis.html) explains the response, equations, held-out model progression, pooled covariate effects with observed-value distributions, descriptive team size, M5/M6 comparisons, and annual-trend sensitivity. M4 starts in 1977 and excludes the 1994–1995 bush-net transition; earlier totals remain descriptive. M5 and M6 are assumption-based sensitivity scenarios, not uniquely corrected abundance trends. Results must be described as adjusted positive-catch intensity until quantitative effort and operated-zero records are substantially more complete.

`07_predict_best_dates.R` uses the adjusted seasonal-timing and moon effects to rank every continuous 14-day window in the 2026–2035 seasons and writes one scheduling figure. Mist, weather and operations are held constant, so it does not forecast annual abundance.

The species-composition workflow is under `scripts/analysis/02_species_composition/`. Its primary analysis starts in 1977: operations evidence shows mainly southern dawn-net catching through 1975, followed by a mixed transition to intensive night netting in 1976. It excludes swallows and martins caught through the separate targeted daytime process and completes species zeros only on positive non-swallow catch dates. Complete seasons are held out to select the multinomial mean structure: a broad long-term season effect, species phenology, and daily conditions. A Dirichlet-multinomial concentration then converts each daily total to a variance-matched effective total, retaining daily composition while preventing thousands of birds caught together from being treated as independent composition observations. Deleting the largest dates tests direct leverage; the full Dirichlet-multinomial remains a distributional sensitivity. Natural splines are deliberately limited to two degrees of freedom for multi-decadal change and three for within-season timing, emphasizing broad biological patterns rather than short wiggles. Annual predictions use one empirically supported within-season window in every season. Species are consistently ordered by total retained catch. The report expresses each species against the equal-weight geometric mean of the 19 focal species and provides all pairwise long-term contrasts, so the three dominant species do not define the reference in proportion to their raw counts. A reference sensitivity figure re-centers the same fitted predictions using the top ten and top five species by catch; it does not test how refitting with a different focal-species list would change estimates. Inference remains relative composition rather than absolute population change, and later lighting and dawn-net changes remain an explicit limitation.

The dedicated migration-phenology workflow is under `scripts/analysis/03_migration_phenology/`. It models each focal species separately and conditions on that species' seasonal total, so annual abundance and any effort multiplier that is constant within a season do not determine passage timing. The likelihood distributes captures among known ringing dates using a smooth date curve, a broad year-by-date change and moon and mist effects. Daily counts are capped at the species-specific 95th percentile to limit mass-fall leverage, and uncertainty is clustered by season. Predictions use a common 12 November–21 December window and reference moon and mist conditions. Median passage (q50) is the primary endpoint; q25 and q75 are a separate secondary family because q10 and q90 depend too strongly on incompletely sampled tails. Primary inference starts in 1977, while a capture-regime-adjusted sensitivity uses 1969–2023. No passage-date trend survives false-discovery-rate correction. The estimand is condition-standardized capture phenology rather than a direct census of all migration over Ngulia.

The age-and-sex workflow is under `scripts/analysis/04_age_sex_demography/`. It starts with initial captures in 1991, excludes the separate targeted swallow and martin process, and defines first-year birds as EURING ages 3/5 versus adults as ages 4/6. Fourteen species meet explicit age-recording and sample-support rules. Daily quasi-binomial models estimate first-year composition with species-specific passage timing, broad multi-decadal change and optionally daily grounding conditions selected by held-out complete seasons. Information from one species-date is capped to prevent mass falls from becoming thousands of demographic replicates; equal-date, full-individual and high-catch-deletion fits test this choice. Annual estimates combine a smooth long-term component with partially pooled season deviations and standardize over common dates and conditions. Sex inference is restricted to adults in the four species with at least 80% adult sex recording, because sexability is strongly age-dependent in the other taxa. All outputs are catch-composition indices rather than direct productivity, survival or population estimates. The [age and sex demography report](outputs/analysis/04_age_sex_demography/age_sex_demography_analysis.html) documents the estimands, literature context, validation, sensitivities and limits.

`data/03_intermediate/` holds source-specific working tables and model artefacts; `data/04_curated/` holds the single canonical datasets used externally. Response-specific model flags and scientific results belong in `outputs/analysis/`; human-facing checks in `outputs/qa/`; external delivery formats in `exports/`.

The former 2014-paper update was a mixed, presentation-oriented script. Its retained figures now sit in the corresponding output folders as `legacy_2014/` (with a `dark_ppt/` variant). They are retained for reference, not treated as current reproducible analyses; each should be recreated in its own analysis when that question is developed.
